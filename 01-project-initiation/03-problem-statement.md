# Problem Statement

## Maji Ndogo Water Services Project

---

## 1. Problem Overview

Access to safe, reliable and efficient water services can be affected by multiple interconnected factors.

The available Maji Ndogo water-services data contains information that can be used to examine these factors, including water sources, population dependence, water quality, waiting times, infrastructure requirements and geographic characteristics.

However, these dimensions need to be analysed together to develop a meaningful understanding of service conditions and potential priorities.

The core problem addressed by this project is therefore:

> **How can available water-services data be transformed into a reliable, integrated and decision-oriented analytical solution that enables stakeholders to identify significant service challenges and prioritise areas for further investigation or potential intervention?**

---

## 2. Current-State Challenge

The available information contains multiple dimensions of water-service performance, but analysing individual variables in isolation can make it difficult to establish the overall level of service challenge within a particular area.

For example:

- A water source may serve a large population.
- A source may simultaneously experience water-quality concerns.
- Communities may experience long waiting times.
- Infrastructure requirements may differ substantially between locations.
- The estimated investment required may vary by intervention type.

Looking at each of these factors separately may not provide sufficient information to support prioritisation.

The project therefore requires an integrated analytical approach.

---

## 3. Impact of the Problem

If water-service information is not effectively consolidated and analysed, decision makers may face challenges such as:

### Limited Visibility

It may be difficult to obtain a consolidated view of water-service conditions across different locations.

### Inconsistent Prioritisation

Without a consistent analytical framework, areas may be difficult to compare objectively.

### Limited Evidence for Decision Making

Decision makers may not have a single view connecting population exposure, water quality, service access and infrastructure requirements.

### Reporting Inefficiency

Manually analysing and presenting multiple datasets can make reporting slower and more difficult to reproduce.

### Limited Scalability

A highly manual analytical process may become increasingly difficult to maintain as datasets and reporting requirements grow.
